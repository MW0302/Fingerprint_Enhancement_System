B and D share one convention (confirmed, as the task described); A and C
each use their own. Deltas are **recomputed uniformly** from each
pipeline's own `stageN` columns (`delta_p1 = stage1 - raw`, `delta_p2 =
stage2 - stage1`, `delta_p6 = stage3 - stage2`) rather than trusted from
each file's own precomputed delta columns — A and C don't have any, and for
B/D the recomputed values were cross-checked against the file's own
`delta_p1`/`delta_p2`/`delta_p6` columns and matched exactly everywhere
both are non-null (the script raises `AssertionError` if they ever
disagree; it didn't).

## Missing-value handling

`DB3_B/110_5.tif` is the **only** row with a missing `raw_nfiq2` in any of
the four files (confirmed by scanning all 320 rows in each) — NFIQ2 itself
can't score that raw image (`FRFXLL_ERR_FB_TOO_SMALL_AREA`). In every
pipeline, `stage1`/`stage2`/`stage3` are still successfully scored for this
image. Consequence: `delta_p1` (needs `raw`) is undefined and this row is
excluded **only** from `delta_p1`'s n (319, not 320) in every pipeline;
`delta_p2` and `delta_p6` don't involve `raw` at all and keep this row
(n=320). No other row in any of the four files has any missing
stage1/stage2/stage3 value. This per-category exclusion (not a blanket
drop, not a zero-fill) falls out automatically from computing deltas off
the real `stageN` columns — no special-casing was needed.

**Read honestly, only 3 of 12 show real, meaningfully-sized signal**
(margin comfortably >10%, R² clearly positive): pipeline_a/delta_p6
(+13.8%, R²=0.256), pipeline_c/delta_p1 (+14.1%, R²=0.290), pipeline_c/

pipeline_a/delta_p2 +3.5%, pipeline_d/delta_p1 +0.2%, pipeline_c/delta_p6
+7.8% borderline).

**Reportable negative finding, not hidden:** **Pipeline B fails the model
check in all 3 of its categories** (R² -0.039, -0.284, -0.223 — baseline
wins every time). The 7 diagnostic features carry no learnable signal for
any of Pipeline B's technique contributions in this dataset. Pipeline D
fails 2 of 3 (delta_p2, delta_p6 both clearly negative) and its 3rd
(delta_p1) is a coin-flip (R²=-0.006 but MAE margin +0.2% — see the caveat
below on why these two metrics can point slightly different ways on a
near-zero result; treat this combination as "no real signal," not "weakly
positive").

*Caveat on pipeline_d/delta_p1's R² vs. MAE disagreement:* R² can be
unstable on small, noisy per-fold validation sets — a negative R² means the
model explains less variance than always predicting the mean (squared-
error terms), while MAE (absolute error) can still land marginally lower
by chance even when R² is negative. Both metrics are reported so this
doesn't get glossed over; the honest reading of this specific row is "no
real signal," not "the model barely wins."

### Selection rule (stated explicitly, applied mechanically, not eyeballed)

1. Rank the 4 pipelines by `mean_delta` for the category — highest wins by default.
2. Reliability check on that leader: flag it if **either**
   - (a) its `%degraded` is more than 15 percentage points worse than the
     runner-up's `%degraded` (i.e. it wins on average partly by regressing
     far more images than the alternative), **or**
   - (b) its `worst_case` magnitude is more than 2× the runner-up's
     `worst_case` magnitude (i.e. a severe single-image failure mode the
     runner-up doesn't share).
3. If flagged, fall back to the runner-up (checked the same way against the
   pipeline after it, not silently auto-resolved).

## Surprises and caveats (the part not to skip)

1. **Model-based and mean-based selection agree on all 3 slots, but this
   wasn't guaranteed and is worth stating plainly rather than assuming.**
   Pipeline C has the only real model signal for P1 and P2 (R²=0.290,
   0.350) and is also the mean-based winner for both. Pipeline A has the
   only real model signal for P6 (R²=0.256) and is also the mean-based
   winner there. Convergence across two independently-computed lenses is
   reassuring, but it's a finding, not a certainty baked into the method —
   a project with different data could easily see these disagree.

2. **P1 is a closer race than the raw ranking suggests.** Pipeline C wins
   on mean (5.05) but only by 0.12 over Pipeline B (4.93) — within noise.
   C's `%degraded` (30.72%) is actually *worse* than B's (24.76%), a 5.96
   percentage-point gap — real, but under the 15pp flag threshold, so the
   rule (correctly, mechanically) doesn't override the mean-based pick.
   Worth a second look by a human before this gets locked into code: C
   also has the wider spread (sd=11.20 vs B's 8.13) and the worse
   worst-case (-28 vs -23). None of this individually crosses the stated
   flag thresholds, but stacked together it's the least clear-cut of the
   three selections.

3. **Pipeline C's P6 (Log-Gabor) is the only *negative* mean-delta in the
   entire table** (-0.14, and barely more images degraded than improved:
   48.44% vs 46.25%). This matches this project's own prior findings
   (Log-Gabor's add_gain sweep never found a combination that made this
   step net-positive dataset-wide) — not a new surprise, but a clean
   confirmation the pattern holds now that all 4 pipelines' real data can
   be compared side by side, and directly relevant to why Pipeline C does
   **not** win the P6 slot despite winning both other slots.

4. **Pipeline B fails the model check in all 3 categories** (Phase A) even
   though it's the mean-based runner-up for P1 and wins nothing outright.
   Its per-image contributions are real (non-zero means, sensible
   improve/degrade splits) but apparently not predictable from these 7
   image-level features at all — worth flagging to whoever owns Section
   2.2's writeup as a clean negative result, not swept under the rug.

5. **Pipeline D never wins a slot and has the weakest overall profile** —
   smallest `sd_delta` in every category (its stages seem to make small,
   consistent changes rather than large ones either way), fails 2-3 of its
   3 model checks, and its `%unchanged` rate is notably higher than the
   other three in P2 and P6 (15.31%, 21.88%) — consistent with a
   conservative, blend-parameter-heavy design (per its own docstrings)
   rather than a bug.
